FAQ: DEVICE SENSORS

1. Why do I need to click "Initialize"? ▼
Apple (iOS 13+) blocks websites from reading your motion sensors unless you explicitly tap a button to grant permission.
2. What is an Accelerometer? ▼
It is a tiny chip with a microscopic weight on a spring. When you move the phone, the weight moves, detecting gravity and speed.
3. Gyroscope vs. Accelerometer? ▼
Accelerometer measures *movement* (up/down/left/right). Gyroscope measures *rotation* (twisting/turning). They work together for stabilization.
4. Why is the Compass wrong? ▼
Magnetometers are easily confused by metal objects (like cases with magnets) or interference. Wave your phone in a "Figure 8" to calibrate it.
5. Does this drain battery? ▼
Yes. Continuously reading high-speed sensor data prevents the processor from sleeping. Close this tab when done testing.
6. Why are the numbers jumping? ▼
Sensors are extremely sensitive. Even your heartbeat or hand tremors can cause tiny fluctuations in the data stream.
7. What is "Proximity Sensor"? ▼
It detects when the phone is near your face (to turn off the screen during calls). Websites currently cannot access this sensor for security reasons.
8. What is a "MEMS" chip? ▼
Micro-Electro-Mechanical Systems. It's basically a microscopic machine with moving parts inside a silicon computer chip.
9. Can websites use this to track me? ▼
Yes. Your specific hand tremors and walking gait can be used to "Fingerprint" you uniquely. That's why browsers now ask for permission.
10. Why doesn't this work on PC? ▼
Desktop computers and laptops usually don't have accelerometers or gyroscopes built in. This test is for mobile devices.
