FAQ: AUDIO & ACOUSTICS

1. Why can't I hear 15kHz? ▼
This is called "Presbycusis." As we age, the tiny hair cells in our ears that detect high frequencies die off. Most people over 25 lose sensitivity above 16kHz.
2. What is "Active Noise Cancellation"? ▼
ANC microphones listen to outside noise and generate an "Anti-Wave" (inverted phase) to cancel it out before it hits your ear.
3. What is "Hz" (Hertz)? ▼
It measures vibrations per second. 60Hz means the speaker cone moves in and out 60 times a second (Deep Bass).
4. Why is phone audio usually bad? ▼
Physics. To make deep bass, you need to move a lot of air. Phone speakers are too small to push enough air for true bass frequencies.
5. What is "Sample Rate" (44.1kHz)? ▼
Digital audio is like a movie (frames per second). 44,100 samples per second is the standard for CD quality audio.
6. How does the Visualizer work? ▼
It uses an FFT (Fast Fourier Transform) algorithm to break down complex sound waves into individual frequency bars.
7. What is "Clipping"? ▼
When the volume is too loud for the speaker to handle, the sound wave gets "chopped" off at the top, creating harsh distortion.
8. Mono vs. Stereo? ▼
Mono is one channel (same sound everywhere). Stereo is two channels (Left/Right), creating a sense of space and direction.
9. Why do I hear static? ▼
Static is usually caused by "Interference" from other electronics or a bad connection in your headphone jack/port.
10. Is louder always better? ▼
No. High quality audio is about "Dynamic Range" (the difference between quiet and loud), not just maximum volume.
